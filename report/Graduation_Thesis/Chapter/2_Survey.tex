Chapter 2 presents a comprehensive survey and analysis of the system requirements. The chapter begins with a status survey to establish the project context, followed by a functional overview that illustrates the system's scope through general and detailed use case diagrams as well as business processes. Subsequently, specific features are examined in depth in the functional description section. Finally, the chapter concludes by outlining the essential non-functional requirements necessary to ensure the system's performance and quality standards.

\section{Status survey}
\label{section:2.1}

In November 2025, a web-based platform called “An toàn mùa lũ” was launched by engineering teams from FPT and Quy Nhon AI. Currently, the system is only available through a website and does not have a dedicated mobile application, which limits user access during emergency situations. Moreover, the platform only allows communication between users and authorities, while communication with family members and friends is not supported. In addition, although emergency rescue is a key focus, post-disaster support and recovery are also important but have not received sufficient attention.

Our system is designed as a cross-platform application to ensure broad accessibility for users across different devices. It enables communication not only with authorities but also with family members, friends, or the wider community, thereby strengthening information sharing and mutual support during emergency situations. In addition, the platform facilitates direct connections between benefactors and relevant authorities, helping to reduce unnecessary administrative procedures and improve coordination efficiency. Furthermore, the system provides tools to manage and monitor the entire charitable donation process in a transparent manner, allowing all stakeholders to track contributions and their utilization clearly and reliably.

\subsection{General use case diagram}
\label{subsection:2.2.1}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering

    \caption{General usecase diagram}
    \label{fig:Fig1}
\end{figure}

The system identifies five main actors: normal user, rescuer, benefactor, authority, and administrator. Each actor plays a distinct role and is granted different levels of access and functionality based on their responsibilities.

A normal user represents the general public using the system to access information, communicate with others, and receive support during emergency situations. This actor forms the foundation of the user base and can utilize all standard features provided by the platform.

A rescuer has all the capabilities of a normal user, with additional access to rescue-related functions. These functions support activities such as reporting rescue operations, coordinating with authorities, and assisting affected individuals in emergency scenarios.

A benefactor also inherits all features available to a normal user and is granted additional functionalities related to charitable activities. This actor can participate in donation processes, support relief efforts, and coordinate with relevant parties to ensure effective and transparent aid delivery.

An authority is responsible for approving and supervising actions performed by rescuers and benefactors. In addition, this actor has the ability to issue official announcements and critical information to other users, ensuring accuracy, coordination, and public safety.

An administrator (admin) is responsible for overall system management. This includes maintaining system stability, managing user accounts and permissions, and ensuring that the platform operates securely and efficiently.

The Authentication and Account Management use case group includes all functions related to user identity verification and account handling. These use cases ensure secure login, registration, role assignment, and profile management for all actors within the system.

The Map use case group covers interactions related to map display and navigation. It supports the visualization of locations, emergency areas, rescue points, and other relevant geographic information to enhance situational awareness.

The Social Interaction use case group focuses on communication and interaction between users. These use cases enable users to connect with family members, friends, rescuers, benefactors, and authorities, facilitating information sharing and community support during both emergency response and recovery phases.

\subsection{Business process}
\label{subsection:2.2.3}

This section outlines the core operational workflows of the application, illustrating the interactions between system actors (Benefactors, Rescuers, Users, and Authorities) and the platform. The business processes are designed to ensure transparency in charity campaigns, accuracy in financial tracking, and efficiency in emergency rescue coordination. The following subsections detail the logic and sequential steps for the primary modules: Charity Campaign Lifecycle, Donation Processing, and Rescue Operations.

\subsubsection{Charity Campaign}

\begin{center}
    \includegraphics[width=1.0\linewidth]{Figure/Charity Campaign.png}
    \captionof{figure}{Charity Campaign} 
    \label{fig:Fig6}
\end{center}

The charity campaign lifecycle begins with the benefactor declaring the campaign details and submitting a formal request to the authority for verification. Once approved, the campaign is authorized to accept donations. Upon the conclusion of the fundraising period, the campaign transitions to the 'Distributing' status. During this phase, the benefactor utilizes the funds to purchase essential supplies or distribute financial aid. Finally, to formally conclude the campaign and ensure transparency, the benefactor is required to upload the bank statement for review.

\subsubsection{Donate}

\begin{center}
    \includegraphics[width=1.0\linewidth]{Figure/Donate.png}
    \captionof{figure}{Donate} 
    \label{fig:Fig7}
\end{center}

The donation process commences when the user selects a target campaign from the list of projects currently accepting contributions. Following this selection, the user downloads a system-generated QR code, which embeds a specific transaction message containing the user's identification details. The process concludes when the user utilizes an external banking application to scan the QR code and execute the fund transfer, ensuring the transaction is automatically tracked by the system.

\subsubsection{Rescue}

\begin{center}
    \includegraphics[width=1.0\linewidth]{Figure/Rescue.png}
    \captionof{figure}{Rescue} 
    \label{fig:Fig8}
\end{center}

The rescue workflow initiates when a user in distress transmits an emergency warning signal. Upon receiving the alert, the Rescuer may respond via two scenarios: deploying immediately to the location or manually selecting a target from the signal list based on severity levels. In the latter case, the Rescuer utilizes the application's optimal route generation feature to plan the most efficient path before proceeding. The process concludes based on the outcome: if the rescue is successful, the victim confirms their status by selecting 'Safe and Sound'; conversely, if the attempt fails, the Rescuer updates the system by marking the status as 'Cannot save'.

\section{Non-functional requirement}
\label{section:2.4}
To ensure the system operates effectively under real-world conditions, particularly in emergency scenarios, specific non-functional requirements regarding device permissions and network performance must be met.

The functionality for sharing weather conditions and coordinating rescue efforts relies heavily on precise geolocation data. Therefore, to utilize the location-sharing feature, it is a mandatory requirement that users explicitly enable Global Positioning System (GPS) or location services on their mobile devices. The system must prompt the user for permission if these services are detected as disabled.

Given that the application is intended for use in flood-affected areas where telecommunication infrastructure may be compromised, the system must prioritize connectivity stability. To maintain functionality in environments with weak or unstable signals, the transmission of critical data will be implemented using the MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport) protocol. MQTT is selected for its lightweight overhead and low bandwidth consumption, ensuring that essential communication remains consistent and efficient even when high-speed internet is unavailable.
